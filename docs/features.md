# ✨ Features

[← Back to README](../README.md) · [Usage](usage.md) · [Settings](settings.md) · [Architecture](architecture.md)

- **Zero-Latency Dictation:** Powered by Groq's `whisper-large-v3` for near-instant speech-to-text. Long dictations stream — chunks are transcribed while you're still speaking, so releasing the key types almost instantly.
- **Voice Commands:** Say "new paragraph", spoken punctuation ("comma", "question mark"), "scratch that", or "all caps … end caps" while dictating — the rule list is editable in Settings.
- **Call Detection:** When Zoom, Teams, Webex, Slack, or a browser call (Google Meet) starts using your microphone, GhostWriter offers to start Meeting Mode — and offers to stop and save the notes when the call ends.
- **Quick Notes (⌃⌥J):** Toggle-dictate a thought from anywhere into a per-day notes file — transcribed, polished, timestamped, with click-to-open notifications.
- **Meeting Templates:** Twelve built-in meeting types — grouped in the picker as **Sales & Customer** (Customer Call, Discovery Call, Solution Demo, Solution Scoping), **Delivery & Project** (Project Kickoff, Planning), **Team & Internal** (Standup, 1:1, All-Hands, Brainstorm), and **Other** (Lecture / Webinar, General) — each extracting their own summary sections, chosen right in the start-meeting dialog. Need something else (interview, retro, …)? Add it as a custom template. Edit any template's **summary sections and follow-up guidance**, or **add your own templates** (custom name + sections + guidance) in Settings.
- **Key-Field Extraction:** Beyond the prose summary, each meeting type pulls the **fields that matter for it** into a **Key Details** section and machine-readable YAML front-matter — a customer call's deal stage, budget, timeline, decision maker, and next step; an interview's recommendation, key strength, and key concern; a 1:1's sentiment and focus-next-time; a planning session's target date and top risk. **Categorical** fields (deal stage, recommendation, sentiment) are also mirrored into `tags:` so you can **filter by them in the Catalog** with the normal tag filter, and every field is Dataview-queryable in Obsidian. On by default (network required); uses the cheap lightweight model.
- **Context-Aware Polishing:** Detects the app you're typing in (e.g. Slack, Mail, Xcode) and uses a Llama model (default `llama-4-scout`, configurable) to format dictation appropriately — casual for Slack, formal for Mail, code-friendly for IDEs. Every writing style is **editable**, you can **add your own styles**, and a global default covers unrecognized apps.
- **Meeting Mode:** Captures system audio via CoreAudio **process taps** (no screen-recording permission required) alongside your microphone, producing a timestamped, speaker-labeled (**You** / _Them_) transcript.
- **Meeting Summaries:** When a meeting ends, an AI summary (TL;DR, decisions, action items) is appended to the notes automatically, and a notification lets you click straight into the file. In Local-only mode the summary is generated on-device (Apple Intelligence) instead. **Long meetings are handled by map-reduce**: rather than clipping to the last stretch (which would drop the opening), the whole transcript is condensed in chunks first — preserving every decision, action, and number — so an hours-long meeting is summarized end-to-end, not just its tail.
- **Sourced Summaries:** Summary points carry the transcript **timestamp** they came from (e.g. "Agreed to ship Friday [00:14:02]"), so you can jump back to the exact moment to verify — and the citation requirement itself keeps the model honest.
- **In-Meeting Bookmarks:** Press **⌃⌥B** during a meeting to mark the moment — an inline `★` marker lands in the transcript, and a timestamped **Bookmarks** jump-list is written at the end so you can find the parts that mattered.
- **Topic Chapters:** Optionally segment a finished meeting into 3–8 titled, timestamped chapters (a `# Chapters` jump-list) so long transcripts are navigable. One extra AI call per meeting; cloud-gated and toggleable.
- **Structured Extraction:** Beyond action items, the summary can pull out **Decisions**, **Risks & Blockers**, and **Open Questions** as their own sections. Toggleable in Settings.
- **Unanswered Questions:** Extracts the questions that were raised in the meeting but never got a clear answer — your ready-made follow-up list — into an **Unanswered Questions** section. One extra lightweight AI call; cloud-gated and toggleable.
- **Keyword Radar:** Keep a **watchlist** of terms — competitors, product names, risk phrases (one per line in Settings) — and every finished meeting is scanned **locally** (works offline) for them. Hits are surfaced in a **Mentions** section with counts and mirrored into `tags:`, so you can filter the Catalog by which meetings mentioned a competitor. No network, no AI cost.
- **Keyword Radar Insights:** A cross-meeting rollup of those watchlist hits, in the **Catalog** (sidebar → Tools → **Keyword Radar**). Each term shows total mentions, how many meetings mentioned it, and when it last came up, ranked by frequency with a proportional bar — click any term to drill into the source meetings and open them. Re-scans note bodies against your *current* watchlist, so terms you add later surface retroactively. Offline, no AI cost.
- **Smart Titles:** Finished meetings get a concise AI-generated `title:` in their front-matter (e.g. "Acme SSO Migration Scoping") instead of a bare timestamp — shown in the viewer and Catalog. The on-disk filename stays `Meeting_<timestamp>.md`, so links never break. Cloud-gated; Local-only keeps the timestamp.
- **Draft Documents from a Meeting:** From any meeting note, draft a document via the **Draft…** menu — the *output document type* is its own axis, separate from the meeting type. The menu leads with **Suggested** documents for the note's meeting type (a Solution Demo suggests Follow-up Email, POC Plan, Proposal), then lists all 13 built-in types grouped as **Email & Recap** (Follow-up Email, Quick Recap, Thank-You Note), **Records** (Minutes of Meeting, Decision Log, Action-Item List), **Sales** (Proposal / Next Steps, POC Plan, Talking Points), **Reports** (Status Update, Executive Summary, FAQ), and **Retro** (Retrospective) — plus your own custom types, or **Auto — match meeting type**, which shapes a follow-up to the meeting template (a customer call → client email, etc.; that per-template guidance is **fully editable** in Settings). Each document type **caches independently**, so one meeting can yield an MoM *and* a follow-up email *and* a status update, and switching between them is free. Each opens in the in-app editor to tweak, copy (as rich text), or save. The drafting guidance for every document type — recipient, tone, sections, format — is **fully editable** in Settings → Meetings → **Draft Templates**, with a per-type reset to the built-in default, and you can **add your own custom document types** there too.
- **Follow-Up Packet (one click):** From any meeting note, the Draft… menu leads with **Follow-Up Packet** — a single grounded document that bundles the three things you send after a call: a **client follow-up email**, an **updated POC plan**, and the **action items**. The POC plan is grounded in the linked opportunity's *current success criteria* (a status snapshot from the Catalog — passed / pending / failed — plus AI next steps focused on the unmet ones); action items reuse the note's own curated `## Action Items` when it has them (no token spend), and are drafted from the transcript otherwise. The three AI sections generate concurrently and each degrades to an inline note if it fails, so the packet always assembles. It opens in the in-app viewer to read, **Regenerate**, copy as rich text, or **export the whole thing as one PDF**. Which sections are included is configurable in Settings → Meetings → **Draft Templates → Follow-Up Packet**. (Cloud AI; hidden in Local-only mode.)
- **Template Import / Export:** Back up or share your customisations as one portable `.json` bundle — your custom meeting templates, custom document types, and any edits to built-in guidance (built-ins themselves aren't included). Import offers **Merge** (add/update by id, keeping your others) or **Replace** (swap all custom template data), mirroring the Catalog's import UX. Lives in Settings → Meetings → **Meeting Templates** (a pane split out of Notes & Summaries so both template editors sit together).
- **Auto-Tagging & Entities:** After summarizing, GhostWriter extracts 3–6 topic tags **plus the named entities the meeting is about** — attendees, customer/client, and project — and writes them into the note's YAML front-matter: mirrored into `tags:` for instant Obsidian/Notion graph links, and as structured `attendees:` / `customer:` / `project:` fields for Dataview-style filtering. On by default. Person names are only harvested when redaction is off, so a privacy-conscious setup never captures them.
- **Live Meeting Assistant:** An opt-in floating brief that rides along while a meeting runs — a rolling TL;DR and the open action items so far, refreshed as the conversation develops (cost-aware: only re-briefs on meaningful new dialogue, and never runs in Local-only mode). **Ask** a grounded question about the meeting so far and get an answer from the live transcript. Click the header to collapse it to a minimal strip, clear the Q&A, or hide it entirely and reopen from the menu. On by default when you're set up for cloud transcription.
- **Agendas, Static & Dynamic:** Type an optional agenda when you start a meeting (comma-separated). The Live Brief panel shows a **coverage checklist** — and *also surfaces topics the meeting itself raised* that you didn't list (marked with a ✨), so the agenda builds itself even when you start with a blank one. You stay in control of completion: items are ticked **by tapping** (the model surfaces topics but never auto-marks them done, so nothing flips green on you); tap a discovered topic's **✕** to dismiss it as noise (it won't come back or reach the notes). The final agenda — planned items and kept discovered ones, each checked or not — is written into the notes as its own **`# Agenda`** checklist.
- **Ask Before It Ends:** When you deliberately end a meeting, GhostWriter checks the agenda and — if anything's still unticked (agenda items or discovered topics you haven't marked done) — offers **Keep Recording / End Anyway** with the list, so nothing important slips through. Reuses the live panel's state when it's running (no extra call); otherwise a one-shot check. Cloud-only and best-effort; skipped for very short meetings and in Local-only mode.
- **Cost Controls:** A **Lightweight-tasks model** setting routes high-frequency background work (live brief, agenda coverage, auto-tagging, search-term expansion) to a cheaper/faster model (`llama-3.1-8b-instant` by default) while summaries, follow-ups, and Ask stay on the polishing model. Set an optional **monthly budget** and GhostWriter tracks month-to-date spend, shows a progress bar, flags `⚠️ over budget` in the menu, and notifies once when you cross it — a soft cap that never blocks transcription.
- **In-App Notes Viewer/Editor:** Open any note in a built-in Markdown editor. Notes open **read-only** by default in a rendered preview with three navigation aids: a **⌘F find** that highlights just the matched word/phrase inline (not the whole line) and steps through matches (next/prev); a **Contents** sidebar — a table of contents listing section headings and, badged with a clock, the meeting's topic chapters, each click-to-jump; and **clickable chapter bullets** in the note body, which scroll straight to that moment in the transcript by matching the chapter's timestamp. The footer keeps everyday actions (edit, contents, find, copy, summarize, draft) one click away and folds the rest (Export PDF, Open in Default App, Reveal in Finder, Rename Speakers) into an overflow menu. Click **Edit** to switch to the raw Markdown, which uses the **native find bar** with **find & replace** (⌘F, ⌘G / ⇧⌘G to step). Then edit and save, or **export to a formatted PDF** — a designed document (styled in the app's own navy/cyan palette) with a title header, a **Properties** box (meeting type, date, organisation/opportunity/project resolved from the Catalog, attendees, tags), a **clickable table of contents** whose entries jump to their section, per-section hairline rules, page-numbered footers, and a **POC Success Criteria** section (with the linked opportunity's criteria) appended at the end and listed in the contents. A **Summarize** button opens the note's AI **brief** — key-point bullets plus **Next Steps** and **Action Items** — in its own window, with a **Regenerate** button to discard the cached copy and produce a fresh one. This is the same structured artifact the Catalog's relationship digest uses (generated once per note, then reused). Works on-device in Local-only mode. Reachable from the menu, the Catalog, and Ask results.
- **Note Viewer:** Notes open in a built-in viewer that **renders the Markdown** when reading — headings, bullet and task lists (with checkboxes), block quotes, code, rules, inline bold/italic/links, and a compact Properties box for the YAML front-matter — and switches to a raw monospaced editor (with ⌘F find & replace) when you unlock to edit. Prefer your own editor? Turn on **Open notes in external editor** (Settings → General) and every note-open — menu, recent list, Catalog, Ask — hands off to your default `.md` app (VS Code, Obsidian, Typora, …).
- **On-Device AI (Apple Intelligence + NaturalLanguage):** Text AI can run entirely on-device. **Summaries, briefs, and follow-up drafts** use Apple's **Foundation Models** LLM (macOS 26+ on capable hardware); **topic/entity tagging** uses macOS **NaturalLanguage** NER (people, organisation, topics), which works on *every* Mac. On-device is used three ways: automatically in **Local-only mode**, as a **fallback** when Groq is unreachable or rate-limited (cloud results seamlessly fall back to Apple), and whenever you opt in with **Prefer on-device AI for summaries & drafts** (Settings → General) — which keeps Groq for transcription while generating text privately and for free on-device. On-device output is tagged "_generated on-device with Apple Intelligence_" so the source is always clear.
- **AI Response Cache:** Note briefs and follow-up drafts are cached to `~/Library/Application Support/GhostWriter/AICache.json`, keyed by the note's content hash + model + prompt version — so reopening a summary or relationship digest costs nothing, and a cosmetic edit (a new tag, reordered attendees) won't force a re-generation. Edits, model changes, or prompt updates invalidate automatically. Regenerate buttons force a fresh copy; **Settings → Usage & Cost → Maintenance → Clear Cache** wipes it. Fully rebuildable, so it's excluded from backups.
- **Local-Only Mode:** A hard privacy switch — transcribe on-device (Apple Speech) *and* run text AI on-device (summaries, briefs, and follow-ups via Apple Intelligence when available; topic/entity tags via NaturalLanguage always). No API cost, and nothing leaves the Mac. Dictation polishing is still skipped, and transcription accuracy is lower than the cloud. Settings shows whether Apple Intelligence is available on this Mac.
- **Auto-Redaction:** Optionally scrub emails, phone numbers, and long number sequences from transcripts before they're typed, saved, or sent to the LLM — replaced with `[redacted …]` labels.
- **Usage & Cost Estimate:** Local counters track audio transcribed and LLM tokens, with a running Groq spend estimate (editable prices) — all-time and month-to-date — in Settings and at a glance in the menu, plus an optional monthly budget with warnings.
- **Error Surfacing & Diagnostics:** Failures post a notification, show a dismissable banner in the menu, and collect in a Diagnostics pane — no more silent gaps.
- **Meeting History & Dictation Recall:** Browse past meetings from the menu bar or the Catalog; ⌃⌥V re-types your last dictation (great when you dictated into the wrong field).
- **Per-Site Browser Styling:** Reads the active browser tab's address (Safari + Chromium; opt-in, Automation permission) so a website gets its own writing style — e.g. Gmail uses Email, GitHub uses Code — via editable `host: style` rules.
- **Dictation Archive:** Optionally save every dictation to its own Markdown file with metadata front-matter (app, host, style, duration, words), organized into dated subfolders (its own layout, independent of meetings). Browse and search them from the menu bar → **Dictations…** — a day-grouped table showing app, style, and duration at a glance, with a **Reload** that re-scans on demand (and on window focus) so dictations deleted on disk drop off.
- **Custom Vocabulary & Replacements:** Feed Whisper your names, acronyms, and jargon, plus post-transcription find→replace rules — domain terms transcribe correctly.
- **Catalog-Aware Transcription:** When a meeting is linked to a Catalog org or opportunity, GhostWriter primes Whisper with that context's proper nouns — the organisation, project, and opportunity names, the people on its recent notes, and the voice identities you've taught — so those names transcribe correctly **from the first mention** (not only after they first appear via self-priming). Cleared when the meeting ends, so it never leaks into the next.
- **Offline Fallback:** If Groq is unreachable, transcription falls back to Apple's on-device speech recognition — dictation keeps working with zero network.
- **Retry Queue:** Meeting segments that fail to transcribe (network blips) are retried automatically with backoff; anything unrecoverable becomes a visible `⚠️ transcription failed` marker in the notes instead of a silent gap.
- **Ask Your Notes:** A multi-turn chat grounded in your meeting archive, opened from the menu. Pick a **scope** — all meetings, a chosen set, or a specific organisation/opportunity — then ask. Relevant excerpts are retrieved with **hybrid retrieval** — blending on-device semantic similarity (meaning) with a BM25 keyword score (exact names/IDs), reranked with an exact-phrase bonus and a recency nudge, and working even when no OS embedding model exists — and each answer **cites** which meeting its points came from, with the source files listed underneath. Semantic search is powered by Apple's local `NLEmbedding` (free, private, no network) and falls back to keyword search when no OS embedding model is available. (Day-by-day browsing, Text/Meaning search, and action-item review with Reminders export live in the **Catalog** and **Today's Digest**.)
- **Catalog:** A lightweight CRM-style organiser that sits *beside* your notes without touching them — a graph of the **organisations, people, projects, opportunities and tags** your meetings are about. Open it from the menu (**Catalog…**). A three-column browser (sections → list → editor) plus a **Map** tab that renders the whole thing as one searchable tree — `Org → Project → Opportunity → Note → {People, Tags}` — with **Expand-all / Collapse-all** controls, auto-expand while filtering, and notes listed newest-first. Every record list (organisations, projects, opportunities, people, tags) has its own **text filter**; projects are grouped by organisation; and inside a note's editor the **opportunity, organisation, people, and tag** pickers all filter too, so assigning is fast even with long lists. Each organisation and opportunity offers a **Summarize relationship** button — a one-tap AI digest across its most recent notes ("where things stand", decisions, open threads, risks) opened in its own window, with a **Regenerate** button to refresh it. Each meeting's block is the *same* cached brief the notes-viewer Summarize produces (one AI call per note, reused for both), so the digest is cheap to reopen. Runs on-device in Local-only mode. Organisations form an **unlimited hierarchy** (each with an editable relationship: Root / Customer / Prospect / Partner / Internal / Other); projects belong to orgs, and opportunities to projects. **Notes are assigned to an opportunity *or* an organisation** (mutually exclusive), and the project → org chain is **inherited automatically**. **People and tags are set per note** — each note owns its own set, picked or created inline like tags (people are independent of orgs; an org's people are simply whoever appears on its notes). **Import notes** pulls in your meeting files (`Meeting_*.md` only). A note's editor is **chip-based**: a **Filed under** row shows the assigned opportunity/org as a removable chip with a single searchable **Assign…** picker (opportunity *or* org); **People** and **Tags** are removable chips with a searchable **Add…** that creates on the fly; a **From this note** section turns words the note suggests into any entity type; and its **action items** tick + export to Reminders (individually or all at once). Search notes with the **Text** / **Meaning** (on-device semantic) / **Ask** (a cloud Q&A scoped to the filtered notes, with cited sources that open in the editor) modes, and refine with a single **Filter** menu — badged with the active count — that folds every facet (org, project, opportunity, person, tag) plus **Unassigned** and **Missing** (file gone from disk) triage presets into one control; active filters show as removable **chips** under the search field. Note rows expose inline **reveal / remove** actions, and the footer offers **Import / reload** and **clean-up** for entries whose Markdown file was deleted (files are never touched). Each organisation and person shows a **relationship timeline** — their notes newest-first, dated and click-to-open — so the whole history is one glance. Starting a meeting linked to an org/opportunity pops a small **prep card** — a non-modal floating panel listing that entity's most recent notes (click any to read the full note while the meeting records). No extra input — it's driven by the link you pick in the Start dialog, and skipped when there's no prior history. **Quick add** builds a whole org → project → opportunity → people → tags chain (with a parent) in one sheet. **Export / Import** the whole catalog as a portable `Catalog.json` — export is a drop-in backup; import offers **Merge** (upsert every record by id, keeping the rest) or **Replace** (swap the entire catalog), never touching your note files. Stored as a single `Catalog.json` in the notes folder — rebuildable, backup-friendly, with a **Purge** action that clears the catalog while leaving your note files intact.
- **POC Tracker:** For technical pre-sales, track a proof-of-concept's **success criteria** against a Catalog opportunity across meetings. It lives in the **Catalog** (sidebar → Tools → **POC Tracker**), since criteria hang off an opportunity: pick the opportunity in the list (each shows its passed/total at a glance), then add the measurable outcomes the POC must prove and cycle each criterion **Pending → Passed → Failed** with a single click as the evaluation unfolds. A progress bar shows passed/total and flags when **all criteria are met**. **Suggest from meetings** reads the opportunity's linked meeting notes and adds the success criteria they mention (deduped, and fully editable/removable afterward) — so you can seed the checklist from what was actually discussed instead of typing it all. State is stored per opportunity in `Catalog.json`, so it persists and travels with the catalog — answering "where's the Acme POC?" in one glance instead of re-reading four notes.
- **Catalog Dashboard:** The Catalog opens on its **Dashboard** by default (sidebar → Overview → **Dashboard**) — a single full-width canvas built for the sales-engineer workflow. A **filter bar** at the top scopes every note-scan insight by **time range** (Today / 7 days / 30 days / 90 days / 6 months / all) and by **account** — a searchable dropdown that lays your organisations out in their **parent → child hierarchy** (indented) for easy navigation, and scopes a chosen account plus its descendants — with a live meeting count, a refresh, and a **Reset** button (appears only when a filter is off-default) that restores the defaults (90 days, all accounts). Below it, an **"At a glance"** strip of catalog KPIs (organisations, open opportunities, active POCs, POC pass-rate, open pipeline by currency, linked notes) sits above the card grid — showing the global snapshot with no filter applied, and re-scoping to the entities touched by a meeting in the window ("At a glance — in range") once a time range or account is selected: a **POC Command Center** (passed/pending/failed criteria, progress, at-risk POCs, and an "at-risk only" toggle), **Relationships** (most-engaged accounts + accounts going quiet), **Activity** (meeting count/time + a 6-week trend chart), **Action Items** (open vs. overdue across recent meetings), **Meeting-Type Mix** (a technical-cycle funnel — Discovery → Demo → Scoping → Kickoff — showing where engagements cluster and where they drop off, plus an "other meetings" breakdown), **Competitive & Product Intelligence** (a Keyword-Radar mentions chart), and **Open Technical Questions** (the unanswered-questions follow-up queue, click-through to the meeting). The catalog snapshot renders instantly; the note-scan insights load in the background, and every card — including the KPI strip and POC command center — re-scopes to the selected range and account once the data lands. Charts use Swift Charts.
- **Integrations (event hooks):** Fire an outbound event the moment a meeting is saved, so you can wire GhostWriter into your own tools. Two opt-in destinations (Settings → System → **Integrations**): a **local script hook** (runs your executable with the event JSON on stdin — no network) and an **outgoing webhook** (HTTPS POST). The payload carries note metadata only — title, date, meeting type, the linked org/opportunity/project, and tags — never audio, and free-text fields pass through your redaction settings first. Both are **suppressed in Local-only mode**.
- **Backup & Restore:** One `.zip` snapshot of *everything* GhostWriter stores — meeting notes, quick notes, dictations, and the Catalog — from **Settings → General → Backup**. Move it to another Mac or keep it as an archive; **Restore** merges a backup back into your current folders (same-name files overwritten) and re-indexes the Catalog against the restored notes. Uses the system `zip`/`unzip`, so it's dependency-free, and restores into your *currently configured* folders rather than the paths baked into the archive.
- **Usage Stats:** Local-only counters — dictations, words typed, meetings recorded, meeting time, plus a Groq cost estimate — shown at the top of the menu and in Settings → Usage & Cost.
- **Echo Suppression:** When you're on the built-in speaker instead of headphones, half-duplex gating stops the remote party's voice (picked up by your mic as echo) from being mislabeled as "You".
- **Voice Diarization (experimental):** Label distinct remote voices (Them / Them 2 / Them 3) by fingerprinting each segment's voice — pitch via autocorrelation plus timbre — and clustering, fully on-device. **Rename Speakers…** gives them real names per meeting: the notes file is rewritten, and a live meeting keeps using the new names.
- **Persistent Voice Identities:** Rename a speaker once and GhostWriter **remembers that voice** — the next meeting where they show up is auto-labeled with their real name (e.g. "Them 2" → "Priya"), before the summary and tags are even written, so names flow everywhere. Fully on-device: each meeting's voice fingerprints are matched against your saved identities; a rename teaches a new one. No enrollment step — it learns from the renames you already do.
- **Ask Your Notes (knowledge-base chat):** A persistent, **multi-turn** chat over your meeting archive, opened from the menu (**Ask Your Notes…**). Ask "What did we decide on pricing with Acme?" or "What are my open commitments to the platform team?"; each answer retrieves the most relevant excerpts with **hybrid retrieval** (on-device semantic similarity blended with a BM25 keyword score, reranked with an exact-phrase bonus and recency; works with or without an OS embedding model) and **cites the meetings** it drew from — each citation showing the matched snippet with your terms highlighted, click-to-open. A **scope selector** narrows the search to *all meetings*, a *chosen set of meetings*, or a specific *organisation/opportunity*. Follow-up questions carry the conversation, so "what about their timeline?" resolves against the previous turn. Runs on Groq, or fully on-device (Apple Intelligence) in Local-only / prefer-on-device mode.
- **Proactive Digest:** An optional scheduled rollup — **daily or weekly** — presented in an **interactive window**: the meetings you held (click to open), your **open (and overdue) action items** gathered from across recent meetings (tick them off in place, with owner/due pills and overdue highlighting), and the **Catalog relationships that have gone quiet** (no contact in N days). A copy is archived as a `Digests/` note, and a click-to-open notification opens the window. Assembled entirely on-device (no tokens). Open it anytime from the menu → **Today's Digest**.
- **Action Items as First-Class Tasks:** End-of-meeting summaries emit structured task lists (`- [ ] <action> — @owner (due: date)`); the Catalog and Today's Digest show them as clickable checkboxes with **owner** and **due-date** pills — marking one done writes `- [x]` back into the notes file. Push open items to **Apple Reminders** individually or all at once, with the due date carried over.
- **Organized Notes:** Meeting files are filed into dated subfolders — `Notes/2026/2026-07/03/` by default; switch to year/month, year, or a single flat folder in Settings. History, search, and stats find files in any layout.
- **Global Shortcuts:** Push-to-talk dictation (hold Right Option), Esc to cancel a dictation, ⌃⌥M to toggle Meeting Mode, ⌃⌥P to pause/resume transcription, ⌃⌥N to open the notes — all system-wide, from any app.
- **Native macOS Integration:** Built entirely in Swift. CoreGraphics event taps for global hotkeys, Accessibility (`AXUIElement`) for text injection.
- **Settings Window:** A System Settings-style sidebar UI — configurable AI models, push-to-talk key, meeting overlay mode, speech-detection thresholds, notes folder, and speaker labels. Everything persists and applies live.
- **Guided Permissions:** A live permission-status panel and menu items to authorize Microphone, System Audio Recording, and Accessibility, plus a one-click **Reset All Permissions** that clears the TCC grants and relaunches for a clean re-prompt.
- **Secure Key Management:** Your API key is verified against the Groq API at setup and stored in the macOS Keychain — never on disk in plain text.
